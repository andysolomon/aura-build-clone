## Hero

- Headline: the outcome the visitor gets, in 10 words or fewer. Not the product category.
  - Write "Close your books in two days, not two weeks." Not "AI-powered accounting software."
- Subhead: who it's for and how it works, in one sentence of 25 words or fewer.
- One primary CTA: verb plus object, 2–4 words ("Start free trial", "Book a demo"). One secondary, lower-commitment CTA ("See how it works").
- Under the CTA, one short line that reduces risk, but only one the brief supports.

## Features as benefits

- Write each feature as: what it does, then what that means for the reader. "Auto-matches receipts to transactions, so month-end is a review, not a scavenger hunt."
- Feature titles: 2–5 words, concrete nouns and verbs.
- Order features by how much the audience cares, not by how they were built.

## Proof

- Use specific numbers: "Saves 6 hours a week" beats "saves time".
- Testimonials name a person, role, and company, and mention a concrete result.
- Use clearly fictional company and person names. Never imply endorsement by real brands or people.
- Never invent certifications, awards, security claims, or guarantees.

## Objections

- Turn the top 5 objections into FAQ questions, written the way a buyer would ask them: "Do I have to switch banks?"
- Answer in 1–3 sentences. Start with yes or no when possible.

## Page flow

- Follow problem, solution, proof, offer, action.
- Repeat the primary CTA every 2–3 sections and in a closing CTA section that restates the headline's promise in new words.
- Pricing: name each plan by who it's for. Highlight one recommended plan. List limits plainly.

## Readability

- Aim for a grade 6–8 reading level. Sentences under 20 words on average.
- Use "you" more than "we".
- Paragraphs: 3 lines or fewer on desktop.

## Do

- Write headlines that make sense without the visuals.
- Make every button label say what happens next.

## Don't

- Don't use "Learn more" or "Submit" as button text.
- Don't stack superlatives: "best-in-class", "revolutionary", "next-generation".
- Don't open with the company's history.
- Don't write fake urgency like countdown timers or "only 3 spots left".
