## Define the voice first

- Before writing copy, decide three voice traits from the brief, each with a "this, not that" pair. For example: "confident, not arrogant", "warm, not cutesy", "clear, not clinical".
- If the brief gives no tone, infer it from the audience: developers get direct and precise, consumers get warm and plain, enterprise buyers get calm and specific.
- Apply the same three traits to every line on the page.

## Voice rules

- Write in second person ("you") and active voice.
- Use contractions: "you'll", "it's", "don't".
- Use sentence case for headings, buttons, and nav. Never title case or all caps for full sentences.
- Write numbers as numerals: "3 steps", "40% faster".
- Use one name for each thing. If it's a "workspace" in the hero, it's a "workspace" in the FAQ.
- Headlines: 8 words or fewer. Supporting paragraphs: 2–3 sentences.
- At most one exclamation mark on the whole page.

## Tone by section

- Hero: confident and concrete. State what changes for the reader.
- Features: practical. Explain what it does and why it matters.
- Testimonials: sound like real people. Vary sentence length, and include a specific detail.
- Pricing: plain and transparent. No clever plan names that hide what's included.
- FAQ: helpful and direct, like a support person who wants to save you time.
- Footer and legal: neutral and brief.

## Microcopy

- Buttons say what happens: "Create workspace", "Get the guide", "Talk to sales".
- Empty and error states are calm and offer a next step.
- Form hints are short and specific: "We'll only email you about your account."

## Banned words

Never use: revolutionary, seamless, cutting-edge, game-changer, unleash, supercharge, empower, leverage, synergy, world-class, next-generation, best-in-class, robust, elevate, unlock.

Replace them with what the product actually does.

## Do

- Read each section aloud in your head. If it sounds like an ad, rewrite it as if explaining it to a smart friend.
- Keep humor light and occasional, at most one playful line per section.

## Don't

- Don't open headlines with "Introducing" or "Meet".
- Don't use jargon the audience wouldn't use themselves.
- Don't switch between "we" and the brand name for the company. Pick one.
- Don't use filler phrases: "In today's fast-paced world", "Look no further".
